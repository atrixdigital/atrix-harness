Executive Summary

Lead with the finding, not the method. Someone who stops after this paragraph should have the answer. What you did belongs later, if at all.

Give credit where it is due before the problems, and mean it — a report that reads as an attack gets dismissed rather than acted on. Then name what matters, in order.

  1. State the fact in the lead-in. Then the consequence, in prose. Then why it matters — against a rule the project set for itself, a cost, or a risk somebody owns.
  2. Rank by what hurts first in production. Twenty findings in file order is a list. Ordered by impact, it is an argument someone can act on.
  3. Name the specific thing. apps/api/src/booking/slots.ts:120, not "the booking logic". Three of twenty-six venues, not "several".
Say what you did not check. Every document has a boundary, and stating it protects the reader from assuming coverage you never had.
