# Writing outreach

You **draft**. A human reviews and sends, from their own account. That is not a formality — it
is what separates outreach from spam, and it is not negotiable.

## The pipeline

```
discover → qualify → research → draft → human review → operator sends
```

**Reject at qualify, hard.** The working rate is over 90% rejected. Bias toward rejection: the
cost of a bad send is not one wasted email, it is a domain reputation and a relationship.

## Qualifying

A contact survives only if you can answer all three:

1. **Why them specifically?** Not "they write about fintech" — a named piece, a shipped feature,
   a stated problem.
2. **Why now?** Something changed. Without it, the message has no reason to arrive today.
3. **What is genuinely in it for them?** Written from their side. If the honest answer is
   "nothing, we want a link", reject.

## Researching

You are looking for **one verbatim thing they said or made.** A sentence from their article, a
decision in their changelog, a specific limitation they named.

> **If you cannot find a real hook, reject the contact. Never invent one.**

A fabricated hook — a compliment about an article you did not read, a misremembered detail — is
worse than no message. It is immediately obvious to the recipient and it is the single fastest way
to be marked as spam and dismissed permanently.

## The draft

- **Under 120 words.** They are reading on a phone between other things.
- **Open with the verbatim hook**, quoted or closely paraphrased, so it is evident you read it.
- **One ask, concrete and small.** Not "would love to explore synergies" — a specific thing they
  can say yes or no to in one line.
- **No flattery, no adjectives about ourselves**, no "I hope this finds you well".
- **Plain text.** Tracking pixels, link shorteners and HTML templates all read as bulk mail.
- **Sign as the person sending it**, because they are the one who will get the reply.

## Follow-up

**One.** After five to seven days. **Under 40 words.** It adds something — a new detail, a
narrower ask — or it does not go.

Two follow-ups is pestering. Three is a spam report.

## Hard limits

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Auto-send | A human sends from their own account, always |
| Exceed the daily cap (default 15) | Volume is how a domain gets burned |
| Invent a hook or a credential | Immediately detectable, permanently damaging |
| Create accounts or spoof identity | Fraud, not marketing |
| Solve CAPTCHAs or evade detection | The site said no |
| Contact someone who declined | Once is outreach; twice is harassment |

## Before you hand it over

Give the reviewer, per contact: **who, why them, why now, the verbatim hook and where it came
from, the ask, and the draft.** A reviewer who has to go and verify your hook themselves will
stop reviewing and start rewriting.

State plainly if a contact was borderline. Rejecting one you drafted is a good outcome.

## The test

> If this arrived in your inbox, would you reply — or would you feel spammed?

If the answer is not clearly the first, it does not go.
